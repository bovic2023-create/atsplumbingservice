Septic System Service: What the Pumping Visit Should Actually Include<h2>
A septic tank separates incoming household wastewater into three zones. The scum layer at the top holds fats, oils, and floating solids. The liquid effluent layer in the middle is the clarified water that flows to the drain field for final treatment. The sludge layer at the bottom holds the settled solids that do not decompose. Pumping removes the accumulated sludge and scum before either layer encroaches on the outlet tee enough to allow solids to escape into the drain field.
The pumping triggers are specific: the bottom of the scum layer within 6 inches of the outlet tee, or the top of the sludge layer within 12 inches of the outlet tee. A service provider who measures these levels at every visit and records the numbers gives you the data to predict when the next pumping is actually due rather than guessing.
The effluent filter at the outlet baffle is the last line of defense against solids reaching the drain field, and it is the most overlooked component in a septic system. A clogged effluent filter can cause slow drains throughout a home served by a septic system without any tank failure or drain field problem. It should be cleaned every three to six months. Home Water Filtration: Matching the Filter to What Is Actually in Your Water<h2>
Not all filters address the same contaminants, and a filter that is certified for one category of contaminant is not certified for others. NSF/ANSI Standard 42 covers aesthetic contaminants: chlorine taste, odor, and sediment. A filter certified only to NSF 42 does not address lead, PFAS, VOCs, or biological contamination. NSF/ANSI Standard 53 addresses health-related contaminants: lead, mercury, volatile organic compounds, and microbial cysts. NSF/ANSI Standard 58 covers reverse osmosis systems that use semi-permeable membranes to reduce nitrates, fluoride, arsenic, and dissolved solids.
Before selecting any filter, the Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) for your water utility identifies which contaminants are present and at what levels. That report is the diagnostic starting point. ATS Plumbing helps homeowners interpret CCR data and select filtration that matches the specific contaminant profile rather than defaulting to whatever system the hardware store promotes most heavily.

Sewer Line Camera Inspection and Trenchless Repair: The Structural Difference<h2>
Clearing a blocked sewer line removes the obstruction. It does not repair the structural condition that caused the blockage. Root intrusion through a cracked clay tile joint, a pipe offset from soil settlement, or a section of deteriorated orangeburg pipe produces recurring blockages at the same location because the structural entry point or failure remains after every cleaning.
A CCTV camera inspection run through the sewer line from the cleanout access identifies what is actually in the pipe: root intrusion at a specific joint, cracks or fractures along a run, pipe offset where sections have shifted out of alignment, or calcite deposits on the pipe walls. The camera finding determines whether the line needs maintenance clearing, structural spot repair, cured-in-place sewer lining (a resin-saturated felt liner cured inside the existing pipe to create a new seamless pipe within the old one), or pipe bursting (a hydraulic process that pulls a new HDPE pipe through the path of the old damaged pipe while fracturing the old pipe outward).

Heat Pump Water Heater Installation: Different Physics, Different Math<h2>
A standard electric resistance water heater converts electricity to heat at a ratio of one-to-one. For every unit of electrical energy consumed, one unit of heat is delivered to the water. A heat pump water heater does not generate heat. It moves heat from the surrounding air into the water. The ratio of heat delivered to electricity consumed, called the Coefficient of Performance (COP), is typically 3 to 4 for a heat pump water heater. For every unit of electrical energy consumed, 3 to 4 units of heat energy are delivered to the water.
This difference translates directly to energy cost. A home spending $600 per year to heat water with a standard electric resistance unit would spend approximately $150 to $200 per year with a heat pump water heater operating in heat pump mode. The installation requires specific space conditions, a condensate drain, and a dedicated 240-volt circuit. ATS Plumbing assesses the installation location before scoping the project and installs heat pump water heaters with the full electrical and drainage requirements addressed.

If lead, PFAS, or nitrates appear at elevated levels, an NSF 53 or NSF 58-certified system is indicated. What is the difference between sewer line cleaning and sewer line repair? Sewer line cleaning removes the material causing the current blockage. Sewer line repair addresses the structural condition that caused the material to accumulate. Root intrusion requires removing the roots and eliminating the structural entry point through pipe lining or repair. A pipe offset requires realignment or bypassing. A collapsed orangeburg pipe section requires replacement. Cleaning alone on a structurally compromised line produces a temporarily cleared pipe that will block again at the same location.
